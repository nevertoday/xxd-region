A friend recommends an app. They've used it for months and can't do without it. You open the App Store and search for the name: two pages of results, nothing. You try the English name. Still nothing. You send them a screenshot, and they write back: "Weird, it's right here on mine."

Neither phone is broken, and the App Store hasn't made a mistake. The difference is the account you're each signed in with. Theirs is a US account; yours is a China mainland one.

## One App Store, different shelves in each place

Every <Term k="Apple 账户" /> belongs to one <Term k="国家或地区" />. When you open the App Store, what you see is that country or region's store.

Which stores an app sits in is the developer's decision. Apple's help for developers says the App Store is available in 175 countries or regions, and a developer can release an app in all of them or pick specific ones. The same page adds that an app may not be available for download or use in some countries or regions because of legal or regulatory requirements ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-your-apps-availability/manage-availability-for-your-app-on-the-app-store)). So your friend's app might be missing from the China mainland store because the developer didn't pick it, or for some other reason, and from the outside you can't tell which. Apple's own advice: if you think an app should be available in your country or region, contact the developer directly ([Availability of Apple Media Services](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118205)).

## The region decides a few other things too

It isn't only apps. An account's country or region also decides these.

**Which Apple services you get.** Apple has an article listing the media services available in each country or region ([Availability of Apple Media Services](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118205)). The United States has the longest list, with Apple Books, Apple TV, Apple One, Apple Fitness+ and Apple News+ on it. China mainland has the App Store, Apple Music and Shazam, and podcasts only if they're free; Apple Books, Apple Arcade, Apple TV and Apple One aren't there, and neither are movie purchases. Türkiye has Apple Arcade and Apple TV, but books are public domain only, podcasts are free only, and Apple One isn't on its list either.

**What currency prices are in.** Take the same iCloud+ plan: as of 2026-11-19, Apple's pricing page lists it in US dollars for the United States, in lira for Türkiye and in yuan for China mainland ([iCloud+ plans and pricing](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108047)). The prices themselves change, because Apple and developers both adjust them, and [step 5](/en/guide/5-subscriptions/) of the full guide covers that.

**Which payment methods you can use.** Apple lists those per country or region as well ([Payment methods that you can use with your Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111741)). China mainland takes Alipay, WeChat Pay, Douyin Pay, most credit and debit cards, and an <Term k="Apple 账户余额" />. The United States takes most credit and debit cards, Apple Pay, Apple Card, Apple Cash, PayPal and the account balance. Türkiye takes most credit and debit cards, mobile phone billing and the account balance, and there's no Apple Pay or PayPal on its list. The same page notes that payment methods are subject to change.

So switching to an account in another region is like walking into a shop in another country: different things on the shelves, prices in a different currency, a till that takes different ways to pay.

## What the terms say: it's where you live

That second half needs a few more words, because an account's region isn't just a setting. Apple's terms give it a name. The Apple Media Services Terms call it your <Term k="居住国家或地区" />: the services are available for your use in your country or territory of residence. The US terms put it plainly: "By creating an account for use of the Services in a particular country or territory you are specifying it as your Home Country." The Turkish and China mainland terms say the same ([US terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/us/terms.html), [Turkish terms](https://www.apple.com/tr/legal/internet-services/itunes/tr/terms.html), in Turkish, [China mainland terms](https://www.apple.com.cn/legal/internet-services/itunes/cn/terms.html), in Chinese).

In other words, the country or region you pick when you sign up is you telling Apple that you live there. Apple's advice to people who move follows the same line: if you don't want to enter a payment method for your new country or region, wait until you're physically there, then create a new Apple Account ([Change your Apple Account country or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118283)).

If you live in the United States or Türkiye, this part is easy: give your own real address. If you live in mainland China and want a US or Turkey account, it's worth getting the next few things straight first.

## The address field: this site won't fill it in for you

Signing up and adding a payment method both ask for an address. This site doesn't give out addresses or teach you how to make one up. The reasons are below, and each comes from Apple's own pages.

**What the terms let Apple do.** The US and China mainland terms both say that if you fail, or Apple suspects that you have failed, to comply with any of the provisions, Apple may terminate the agreement and your Apple Account without notice to you ([US terms](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/us/terms.html), [China mainland terms](https://www.apple.com.cn/legal/internet-services/itunes/cn/terms.html)). The Turkish terms are narrower: they cover failing basic obligations such as paying, or fraud ([Turkish terms](https://www.apple.com/tr/legal/internet-services/itunes/tr/terms.html)).

**What Apple actually does.** The terms don't spell out what happens when an account's region doesn't match where you really live, and Apple hasn't said publicly how it handles that. Apple's pages don't say; if you're unsure, ask Apple Support first.

**Your billing address has to match your bank's.** When you add a payment method, Apple asks you to confirm that your name, billing address and other information are spelled correctly and match what you have on file with your financial institution, such as the bank that issued your card ([Add a payment method to your Apple Account](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118429)). When a payment method is declined, Apple again says your billing details must match the information on file with your financial institution ([If your payment method is declined in the App Store or iTunes Store](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108095)). So an address that isn't yours won't match your bank's records on the day you go to add a card.

Put those side by side, and the decision is yours. This is a matter of the terms between you and Apple; this site explains what the terms say, and it isn't legal advice.

## The next two lessons

Now you know what the region decides and what the terms say about it. But when you actually go to use an account in another region, what worries most people isn't the region. It's whether it could hurt the main account you've had for years: your photos and contacts are in its iCloud, and the apps you've bought are in its name.

The next two lessons are about the two things that keep that main account safe. [Lesson 2](/ru/start/2-two-sign-ins/) explains that iCloud and the App Store are two separate sign-ins on your iPhone, and the other-region account goes only in the second one. [Lesson 3](/ru/start/3-new-account/) explains why making a second account is simpler than moving your main one to another region. Everything beyond the three lessons is there to look up when you need it.

## If something's off

<Q q="I don't know which region has the app I want.">

The developer decides which countries or regions an app is released in, so an app missing from the China mainland store isn't necessarily in the US or Turkish one either ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-your-apps-availability/manage-availability-for-your-app-on-the-app-store)). The surest way is to check the app's own website or ask the developer, which is also what Apple suggests ([Availability of Apple Media Services](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118205)). Find out where the thing you want is offered before you choose a region; [step 0](/en/guide/0-why/) of the full guide is about exactly that.

</Q>
<Q q="I don't know which region my account is in right now.">

See [Check which region an account is in](/en/howto/check-region/). It takes a few taps.

</Q>
<Q q="A tutorial says you can type in any address.">

This site doesn't teach that. Your billing address has to match what your bank has on file, and the terms treat the account's region as where you live; both are covered above. If you come across that kind of tutorial, compare it with [Tutorials you shouldn't follow](/en/howto/bad-tutorials/).

</Q>
<Q q="I heard subscriptions are cheaper in some region, so I want to sign up there.">

This site doesn't compare prices or say which region is cheaper. Developers and Apple set the prices, and they change: a developer can raise a subscription's price, some increases need your consent first and Apple contacts you by email or push notification to ask, and others only notify you ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)). So choose a region by the apps and services you'll actually use. [Step 5](/en/guide/5-subscriptions/) of the full guide covers subscriptions and price rises in detail.

</Q>
<Q q="I'm going to move to the United States or Türkiye.">

Then your Home Country changes. Apple's advice is that if you don't want to enter a payment method for the new country or region, you wait until you're physically there and then create a new Apple Account ([Change your Apple Account country or region](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118283)). If you'd rather move your current account over, there's quite a lot to clear first, and [lesson 3](/ru/start/3-new-account/) goes through it.

</Q>

This is a matter of the terms between you and Apple; this site explains what the terms say, and it isn't legal advice.

</Q>
